# Neighborly Mercy: A Sunday School Lesson

## Lesson Overview

<!-- source: lesson_plan -->

- **Big idea:** mercy notices a need, moves toward it, and offers practical care.
- **Text:** Luke 10:25–37, read from the congregation's preferred translation during class.
- **Outcome:** learners will name one barrier to compassion and choose one concrete act of neighborly care for the coming week.

> Teaching frame: the lesson is not about identifying the minimum circle of people we must love. It asks what it looks like to become a neighbor when a need is in front of us.

## Discussion prompts

1. Which character in the story is easiest for you to understand? Why?
2. What might the two passersby have been afraid of losing?
3. What did mercy cost the helper in time, attention, and resources?
4. Who is easy for our group to overlook?
5. What is one small act of care we can complete this week?

## Facilitator notes

- Invite answers without forcing personal disclosure.
- Keep political or demographic examples from becoming abstractions; return to practical care.
- If children are present, let them draw or act out the sequence: notice, approach, help, follow through.
- Close by allowing thirty quiet seconds for each learner to choose a realistic weekly practice.
