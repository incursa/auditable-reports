Executive Summary

  • The portfolio is forecast $850K over its $24.3M approved budget. The current $25.15M forecast is 3.5% above plan.
  • Three projects explain almost the entire gap. Northstar, Atlas, and Cedar contribute $820K, or 96% of the forecast overrun.
  • Recovery attention should stay concentrated. Five of eight projects are stable, while the three at-risk projects are also the three largest unfavorable variances.

Three projects drive 96% of the projected overrun

Northstar (+$350K), Atlas (+$280K), and Cedar (+$190K) account for $820K of the $850K portfolio variance. That concentration makes project-level recovery plans more decision-useful than a broad portfolio-wide reduction target.

Synthetic Project Portfolio Health

Stable projects provide little offset to the three exceptions

Harbor and Willow are forecast $40K and $20K under budget, respectively. Their combined $60K favorable position offsets only a small share of the $910K unfavorable variance across the other six projects, so the net portfolio result remains $850K over budget.

Recommended next steps

  1. Require Northstar, Atlas, and Cedar owners to document the cost drivers, recovery actions, and decision dates behind their current forecasts.
  2. Revalidate the three forecasts at the next close before applying portfolio-level contingency.
  3. Monitor Harbor and Willow so their favorable positions are not used as offsets until the remaining scope and risk are confirmed.

Further questions

  • How much approved contingency remains outside the project budgets?
  • Which portion of each at-risk variance is committed versus estimated?
  • Are any schedule delays likely to create additional indirect cost exposure?

Caveats and assumptions

This is a synthetic fixture designed to demonstrate the report package. It contains one current snapshot, not a historical trend, and risk labels are illustrative. Do not use these values for an operational decision.
